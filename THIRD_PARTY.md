# Third-party software, data and source provenance

The Research Environment simulator bundles independently licensed programs and libraries, and uses others that the browser loads at run time. Each keeps its own licence: the MIT licence of the Biowasm build framework does **not** relicense the programs it compiled, and none of the licences below applies to the simulator's own text and code. Keep each component's notices and licence files when copying or redistributing its files.

Most of what is listed here – the command-line programs, the engine of the terminal, RStudio and Spyder, and the libraries – was copied from the bioinformatics workbench ([comparativechrono/bioinformatics-workbench](https://github.com/comparativechrono/bioinformatics-workbench), commit `3f656f0`); see [assets/ENGINE.md](assets/ENGINE.md). The variant interpreter (`sites/variant-interpreter/`) is a copy of [comparativechrono/variant-interpreter](https://github.com/comparativechrono/variant-interpreter) with its own [THIRD_PARTY.md](sites/variant-interpreter/THIRD_PARTY.md) and [LICENSE](sites/variant-interpreter/LICENSE).

Genomics England, the 100,000 Genomes Project, LabKey, IGV, RStudio, Spyder, Xubuntu, Xfce and IBM Spectrum LSF are names of their owners. The simulator imitates how these look and behave so that what trainees learn transfers to the real Research Environment; it contains none of their code (except the open-source libraries listed below), it is not made or endorsed by any of them, and it holds no participant data.

## Command-line programs (WebAssembly, run in the browser)

These are the real programs, compiled to WebAssembly. They run on the trainee's own computer, inside the web page – in the Terminal, in RStudio's Terminal and in the jobs of the simulated HPC (`bsub`). Each folder `assets/vendor/biowasm/NAME/VERSION/` holds the program (`.wasm`, its JavaScript loader, sometimes a `.data` file), its licence, a `NOTICE.txt` (origin and local changes) and `SHA256SUMS`. (The folder of the base module has a notice and checksums only: its licence is the Biowasm licence in `assets/vendor/biowasm/LICENSE.txt`. The four files at the top of `assets/vendor/biowasm/` – the runner, its licence and the two descriptions of the local changes – have a `SHA256SUMS` of their own there.) The simulator bundles a subset of the workbench's programs.

Every `.wasm` file also contains parts of the Emscripten runtime and its C and C++ libraries (musl, libc++, compiler-rt: MIT; Apache 2.0 with LLVM exception), and, where compressed files are read, zlib (HTSlib-based programs also bzip2 and liblzma). **Where the builds came from.** All programs except Bowtie 2, BWA, HISAT2, FreeBayes, BWA-MEM2, StringTie, kallisto, mosdepth, PLINK 2, `diff` and `cmp`, and the grep that carries out `grep -P` are builds of the [Biowasm](https://biowasm.com) project, downloaded from `https://biowasm.com/cdn/v3/NAME/VERSION/` between 30 September and 10 October 2026 (each `NOTICE.txt` has the date). Bowtie 2, `diff` and `cmp` of GNU diffutils, and GNU grep 3.11 with PCRE2 10.42, were compiled with Emscripten for the workbench – Bowtie 2 from its release source with a patch for the WebAssembly build (its Makefile; the search runs in the calling thread; a read file that cannot be opened is reported), `cmp` from its release source with a one-line patch, `diff`, grep and PCRE2 from theirs unchanged; they are not Biowasm builds. BWA, HISAT2 and FreeBayes were compiled with Emscripten 3.1.64 by the [genomics-wasm](https://github.com/comparativechrono/genomics-wasm) project (commit `0e5df1fb`; its scripts: MIT), from pinned sources with small portability patches, and checked against native builds of the same sources by its comparison suite; for the workbench they were linked for the program runner (the `.wasm` files are those that were checked) and FreeBayes was compiled again with its source paths shortened. BWA-MEM2, StringTie, kallisto and mosdepth come from the same project (commit `e8c3e15`), compiled with its scripts – with the build machine's folders mapped out of the strings in the programs (`-ffile-prefix-map`) – checked with its comparison suites against native builds of the same sources, and linked for the program runner (the `.wasm` files are those that were checked). PLINK 2 comes from the same project too (commit `8eea96b`, its PLINK 2 port `d68433f`): a development version of PLINK 2 of 12 October 2026, built without LAPACK with Emscripten 3.1.64 (with `-ffile-prefix-map`) and checked with the project's comparison suite against the native programs (41 of 41 cases); it runs as it was built, each command in a worker of its own (`assets/js/threads-worker.mjs`, adapted from genomics-wasm's runtime, MIT), with threads – which need a cross-origin isolated page. How each was built is in [assets/vendor/licenses/](assets/vendor/licenses/) (`bowtie2-build/`, `diffutils-build/`, `grep-perl-build/`, `genomics-wasm-build/`).

**Local changes** – all listed in [assets/vendor/biowasm/RUNTIME-PATCHES.txt](assets/vendor/biowasm/RUNTIME-PATCHES.txt):

- The JavaScript loaders of the programs record each program's exit status. The Aioli runner has two added methods: `execIO` runs a program with real standard input, output and error – and starts every program afresh for each run, as a process is on Linux –, and `statTree` lists the shared file system in one answer; [execio-source.txt](assets/vendor/biowasm/execio-source.txt) is their readable source.
- In each of the 17 **coreutils** programs and in **grep** 3.7, **one byte** of the `.wasm` file is changed, which makes the C library's standard output unbuffered. Unchanged, these builds lose output that does not end with a newline (`tr`, `head -c`, `fold` …; `grep -lZ`, `grep -z`). The byte, the reason and the checksums of the files as downloaded are in the [coreutils notice](assets/vendor/biowasm/coreutils/8.32/NOTICE.txt) and the [grep notice](assets/vendor/biowasm/grep/3.7/NOTICE.txt); the script that makes the change is [unbuffer-stdout.py](assets/vendor/licenses/local-patches/unbuffer-stdout.py).
- In the loaders of **date** and **gawk** the day of the year is counted in calendar days ([fix-day-of-year.py](assets/vendor/licenses/local-patches/fix-day-of-year.py)): as downloaded, `date -d 2024-07-15` printed “invalid date” in zones with summer time.
- All other `.wasm` and `.data` files from Biowasm are unmodified.

## Loaded at run time, not bundled

- **Pyodide 0.29.5** (MPL 2.0) – CPython 3.13 compiled to WebAssembly – is downloaded by the trainee's browser from the jsDelivr CDN (`https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/pyodide/v0.29.5/full/`) when Python is first used (`python` in the Terminal, or Spyder), and then taken from the browser's cache. It provides CPython (PSF licence) and the Pyodide builds of NumPy, pandas, Matplotlib and the other packages that code imports, each under its own licence (see the [Pyodide package list](https://pyodide.org/en/stable/usage/packages-in-pyodide.html)). Packages installed with `%pip` come from PyPI.
- **webR 0.6.0** – R 4.6.0 compiled to WebAssembly – is downloaded from `https://webr.r-wasm.org/v0.6.0/` when R is first used (`R` or `Rscript` in the Terminal, or RStudio). The webR binaries are distributed under the GNU GPL 3 (R itself: GNU GPL 2 and 3); webR's JavaScript is MIT-licensed ([webR's licence notes](https://github.com/r-wasm/webr/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). R packages come from the webR repository (`repo.r-wasm.org`) and r-universe (`bioc-release.r-universe.dev`, `cran.r-universe.dev`), each under its own licence.
- The **hosted genomes** of IGV (hg38, hg19) are described by the IGV team (`igv.org`), and their files come from there and from UCSC (`hgdownload.soe.ucsc.edu`).
- The **variant interpreter** queries public web services (Ensembl, gnomAD, ClinVar, UCSC and others) under their own terms; see its [THIRD_PARTY.md](sites/variant-interpreter/THIRD_PARTY.md).
- **Google Apps Script** (optional, off by default): when `CONFIG.submissions.mode` is `appsScript`, Airlock requests and research proposals are sent to the instructor's own Apps Script web app ([apps-script/](apps-script/)).
